Section 1.1

How the Google Ads Auction Works

Every time someone types a query into Google, a real-time auction runs invisibly in milliseconds. Understanding this auction at a mechanical level is the single most important thing you can do to lower costs and improve position — because Google rewards relevance just as much as budget.

The Real-Time Bidding Process

Google does not simply give the top ad spot to whoever pays the most. Each auction evaluates every eligible advertiser simultaneously and computes an Ad Rank score. The advertiser with the highest Ad Rank wins the top position. This happens in roughly 100 milliseconds — every single search, every single time.

What makes this system powerful (and occasionally maddening) is that a smaller advertiser with a tightly-written ad and a fast landing page can outrank a large advertiser spending ten times more, simply because their relevance score is higher. This is the foundational asymmetry that makes Google Ads a skill game, not just a budget game.

The Ad Rank Formula

Ad Rank = Max CPC Bid × Quality Score × Expected Impact of Extensions & Formats // Google does not publish the exact weights, but these three factors are confirmed

Each factor matters independently and they multiply — not add — which means weakness in any single area compounds. A QS of 3 with a $5 bid produces a lower Ad Rank than a QS of 9 with a $2 bid.

The Second-Price Auction

Google runs what economists call a Vickrey-style (second-price) auction. You do not pay your maximum bid — you pay just enough to beat the advertiser directly below you. The actual cost per click is calculated as:

Actual CPC = (Ad Rank of the next advertiser ÷ Your Quality Score) + $0.01 // You always pay less than your maximum bid
Worked Example — Auction with 3 Bidders
AdvertiserMax BidQuality ScoreAd RankPositionActual CPC
You€2.00918.01st 🥇€1.24

You win position 1 with a €2.00 bid but only pay €1.24 — because that's what it costs to beat Competitor A's Ad Rank of 14.0 divided by your QS of 9, plus €0.01. Competitor A spends more per click AND gets a worse position.

Ad Position and the SERP Layout

Google can show up to 4 ads at the top of the search results page and 3 ads at the bottom. Top-of-page ads consistently receive 2–4× higher click-through rates than bottom ads. However, top position is not always the most profitable — the auction dynamics mean positions 2 and 3 often deliver better ROAS because you're still prominent but paying significantly less per click.

The ad positions are labeled in the Google Ads interface as Top impression share (percentage of auctions where your ad appeared at the very top) and Absolute top impression share (position 1 specifically). Chasing 100% absolute top impression share is almost always a budget-wasting vanity metric — aim for top 3 with efficient CPC instead.

Auction Eligibility — Thresholds and Reserves

Not every bid enters every auction. Google applies a dynamic minimum bid threshold per keyword that fluctuates based on historical auction competitiveness, the expected quality of the landing page, and the search query's context. If your combination of bid and QS doesn't clear the floor, your ad simply won't show — even if you theoretically bid €10.

This is why new campaigns sometimes show very low impression volume in the first 24–48 hours despite high bids. The algorithm is learning your QS baselines and calibrating your threshold. Give campaigns at least 72 hours before drawing any conclusions about delivery.

Contextual Signals in the Auction

Beyond your keyword bid and QS, Google factors in real-time contextual signals for each individual auction:

  • Device — mobile vs. desktop vs. tablet (you can set bid modifiers per device)
  • Location — physical location of the searcher, and location mentioned in the query
  • Time of day / day of week — you can apply bid adjustments by hour and weekday
  • Audience membership — whether the user is in a remarketing list, Customer Match list, or in-market segment
  • Browser and operating system — minor signal, not directly adjustable
  • Search partner network — some auctions occur on Google partner sites, not Google.com directly

Smart Bidding strategies (Target CPA, Target ROAS) incorporate all of these signals automatically into real-time bid adjustments. Manual CPC bidding ignores most of them unless you explicitly set modifiers.

💡
The compounding effect of Quality Score

A Quality Score improvement from 5 to 8 effectively makes every €1 you bid worth €1.60 in auction power. This means investing time in ad copy, keyword grouping, and landing page relevance often yields better returns than simply raising bids — and it's sustainable because your competitors can't see or copy your QS.

Deep Dive — Search Campaigns

Search campaigns are the backbone of almost every profitable Google Ads account. They put your ad directly in front of someone who is actively typing a query that signals buying intent. No other ad format matches this level of intent alignment.

A Search campaign is organized as: Account → Campaign → Ad Group → Keywords + Ads. The tighter this hierarchy is organized around a single theme, the better the Quality Score, the lower the CPC, and the higher the conversion rate. Dumping 200 keywords into a single ad group is one of the most common and costly mistakes new advertisers make.

Deep Dive — Performance Max (PMax)

Performance Max is Google's most automated campaign type, designed to serve ads across every Google channel simultaneously. It uses machine learning to decide where, when, and to whom to show your ads based on your conversion data and the creative assets you provide.

The catch: PMax requires substantial conversion data (Google recommends 50+ conversions/month, ideally 100+) to make the algorithm meaningful. Before that threshold, the AI essentially guesses, often wasting budget on Display and YouTube impressions that never convert. It also has a known tendency to serve brand search terms that would have converted organically anyway, making its reported ROAS misleading.

🚫
PMax brand cannibalization

If you run PMax alongside Search campaigns, PMax will often bid on your brand keywords — traffic you would have gotten for free or at very low CPC. Always add your brand as a negative keyword list to PMax campaigns, or create a separate brand Search campaign with higher priority settings.

Campaign Structure Best Practices

Organize campaigns by intent theme, not by budget. Each campaign should have a single, coherent goal with a dedicated landing page. Do not mix branding and lead generation into the same campaign — their bidding strategies and KPIs are fundamentally different.

Section 1.3

Match Types — Broad, Phrase, Exact

Match types are the gatekeepers of your budget. They determine which real-world search queries are eligible to trigger your ads. Getting match types wrong is the fastest way to burn a Google Ads budget on traffic that will never convert.

The Three Active Match Types

Google deprecated Broad Match Modifier (BMM) in 2021 and merged its behavior into Phrase Match. As of 2024, there are three active match types: Broad, Phrase, and Exact.

Match TypeSyntaxGoogle triggers your ad for…Risk level
Broad Match website design Synonyms, related concepts, paraphrases — Google decides. "web page creation", "build a site", even "logo design" are all possible. High
Phrase Match "website design" Queries that contain your phrase in meaning, in order. Extra words before or after are OK. "affordable website design UK" would trigger. "design a website yourself" might not. Medium
Exact Match [website design agency] Only that query, or queries Google considers "same meaning" (close variants). Function words can be reordered. Plurals and misspellings match. Low

Close Variants — The Hidden Expansion

Since 2019, Google has progressively expanded what qualifies as a "close variant" for Phrase and Exact match types. This includes:

  • Misspellings and abbreviations (wbsite design matches [website design])
  • Singular/plural forms (websites matches [website])
  • Function word reordering (design for website matches [website design])
  • Implied words (shoes store London might match [shoe store London])
  • Same-meaning queries — the most controversial expansion. Google can decide "web developer" has the "same meaning" as "website design agency" and serve your ad.
⚠️
Exact Match is no longer truly exact

This is the most common source of budget surprises for experienced advertisers who haven't run Google Ads recently. You must actively mine the Search Terms report weekly and add irrelevant variants as negatives — even for "Exact Match" campaigns. Treat Exact Match as "high-precision Phrase Match," not as a locked filter.

Broad Match — The Right Conditions

Broad Match has gotten a bad reputation because it was historically run with Manual CPC bidding, which meant Google distributed your budget across hundreds of irrelevant queries with no optimization signal. In 2023–2024, Google redesigned Broad Match to work specifically in tandem with Smart Bidding.

When you combine Broad Match with Target CPA or Target ROAS, Smart Bidding uses your conversion history to automatically suppress bids on irrelevant queries and increase bids on patterns that resemble past conversions. In this configuration, Broad Match becomes a legitimate discovery tool — one that can surface profitable query variations you'd never think to add as Exact Match keywords.

The Modern Match Type Strategy
Use Exact Match for your 10–20 highest-intent, proven keywords (protect your budget). Use Phrase Match as the workhorse — broad enough to catch variations, specific enough to filter obvious junk. Add Broad Match only after the campaign has 30+ conversions and you've switched to Smart Bidding — use it as a discovery layer with tight negative keyword coverage. Check the Search Terms report weekly to mine new Exact Match additions and negatives.

There are three main philosophies for organizing keywords into ad groups:

The Search Terms Report — Weekly Workflow

The Search Terms Report (found under Keywords → Search Terms in the Google Ads UI) shows the actual queries that triggered your ads. This is your most important ongoing optimization tool.

  1. Open Search Terms report weekly

    Filter by date: last 7 days. Sort by cost descending.

  2. Identify irrelevant high-spend queries

    Any query spending budget with 0 conversions and no obvious intent match is a candidate for negative.

  3. Add irrelevant queries as negative keywords

    Use Phrase Match negatives for intent clusters (e.g. -"free"), Exact negatives for specific bad queries.

  4. Mine for new Exact Match additions

    Any query converting well that isn't already an Exact Match keyword should be promoted — this locks in that traffic and lets you control its bid.

  5. Document changes

    Log every negative and new keyword addition with the date and reason. This prevents accidentally removing negatives later and provides audit trail for clients.

Quality Score & Ad Rank

Quality Score is Google's 1–10 rating of the relevance and quality of your keyword, ad copy, and landing page combination. It directly controls what you pay per click and whether your ad appears at all. It is arguably the most under-optimized lever in most Google Ads accounts.

The Three Components

Expected CTR
~40%
Landing Page Experience
~39%
Ad Relevance
~21%

Weight estimates based on industry analysis — Google does not publish official weights.

Expected CTR — The Most Impactful Component

Expected CTR measures how likely Google thinks users are to click your ad when it appears for a given keyword. It is benchmarked against competitors showing for the same keyword — not an absolute score. An "Above Average" CTR means you're outperforming other advertisers for that keyword.

The most reliable ways to improve Expected CTR:

  • Include the keyword in Headline 1 — Google bolds matching words, improving visual salience
  • Use numbers in headlines: "7 Websites We Built for Law Firms" outperforms "Websites for Law Firms"
  • Add urgency or specificity: "Get a Proposal in 24h" vs. "Contact Us Today"
  • Use all available headline and description slots in Responsive Search Ads
  • Pin your strongest proven headline to position 1 to prevent Google serving weaker combinations
  • Include emotional triggers relevant to the industry (trust, speed, ROI, risk reduction)

Landing Page Experience

Google evaluates your landing page on several dimensions: content relevance to the keyword, page load speed, mobile friendliness, ease of navigation, and how much the page buries users in popups, redirects, or autoplay video. A slow or confusing landing page destroys QS regardless of how good your ads are.

✅
Use dedicated landing pages, not your homepage

Sending Search traffic to your homepage is one of the most common QS mistakes. Your homepage serves many purposes — it cannot be optimized for a specific keyword. Create a dedicated page for each ad group theme (e.g. /architects, /law-firms) with the keyword in the H1, a matching headline, and a single conversion-focused CTA. QS improvements of 2–4 points are common from this change alone.

Ad Relevance

Ad Relevance measures how closely your ad copy matches the intent of the keyword being bid on. Google rates it as Above Average, Average, or Below Average. The most common reason for Below Average ad relevance is placing too many unrelated keywords into the same ad group — making it impossible to write a single ad that's relevant to all of them.

Fix: restructure ad groups around a single intent theme and write ad copy that directly mirrors the keyword language. If your keyword is "law firm website design," your Headline 1 should contain those exact words or a very close variation.

Tracking Quality Score Over Time

QS is calculated live at auction time but the number displayed in the UI is a historical snapshot. The UI only shows QS for keywords with sufficient impression volume — low-traffic keywords show N/A. To track QS trends, use the "Segment by keyword" view and download weekly reports, or use Google Ads scripts to log QS to a Google Sheet automatically.

Important: QS can go up or down without you changing anything. Competitor ads, seasonal search behavior, and Google's own algorithm updates all affect it. A 1-point drop over a month is usually noise; a sustained 2+ point drop warrants investigation.

Section 1.5

Bidding Strategies

Bidding strategy determines how Google spends your budget and optimizes for your goal. Choosing the right strategy for your account's maturity level is critical — a strategy perfect for a 200-conversion/month account will actively harm a new campaign with 5 conversions.

The Strategy Maturity Ladder

Think of bidding strategies as a progression matched to your conversion data volume:

StrategyData RequirementGoogle optimizes forYou control
Manual CPC None — works day 1 Nothing — you set every bid manually Max CPC per keyword, bid adjustments
Enhanced CPC (eCPC) 5+ conversions/month Clicks statistically more likely to convert Base CPC — Google adjusts ±30%
Maximize Clicks None Volume of clicks within daily budget Daily budget, optional max CPC cap
Target Impression Share None Appearing for a target % of auctions Impression share goal, max CPC cap
Maximize Conversions 10+ conversions/month recommended Most conversions within budget Daily budget — no CPA control
Target CPA 30–50 conversions/month minimum Conversions at or below your CPA target Target cost-per-conversion
Target ROAS 50+ conversions/month, known revenue values Revenue relative to ad spend Target return on ad spend (e.g., 400%)
Maximize Conv. Value 10+ conversions with values assigned Highest-value conversions first Daily budget, optional ROAS floor

Manual CPC — When and How

Manual CPC gives you complete control: you set a maximum bid per keyword, and Google will not exceed it. This is the right choice when you're starting with zero conversion data, when you're running a very small test campaign, or when you're doing competitor conquesting where Smart Bidding would over-bid on brand terms.

The downside of Manual CPC is that it ignores all real-time contextual signals — device, time, location, audience membership. A €2.00 bid is €2.00 whether someone's searching on a mobile phone at 11pm (unlikely to convert) or on a desktop at 2pm (much more likely). You have to manually set bid adjustments for each dimension, which requires constant monitoring.

Smart Bidding — The Learning Period

When you switch to any Smart Bidding strategy (Maximize Conversions, Target CPA, Target ROAS), the campaign enters a learning period that typically lasts 1–2 weeks. During this time, Google is testing different bid levels across the contextual signal space to understand what drives conversions for your specific account.

During the learning period you will typically see:

  • Erratic CPA — much higher or lower than your target
  • Fluctuating impression volume and spend
  • "Limited — Learning" status in the campaign view
  • Conversion volume sometimes drops before recovering

Do not panic-change settings during the learning period. Every time you change a Smart Bidding target, budget, or add/remove significant keywords, the learning period resets. The most common mistake is making rapid changes and preventing the algorithm from ever converging. Lock in settings for at least 2 weeks before evaluating performance.

💡
The "Maximize Conversions → Target CPA" bridge

For new campaigns, start with Maximize Conversions (no CPA target). Let it run for 30 days and accumulate 30–50 conversions. Look at your actual average CPA, then set a Target CPA at 110–120% of that number. Tighten it by 10% every 2 weeks as the algorithm stabilizes. This prevents the campaign from going dark because the CPA target is too aggressive on day one.

Target CPA — Setting It Correctly

Target CPA tells Google: "I want to acquire leads at this cost. Spend as much as possible within this constraint." The algorithm will raise bids aggressively when a user matches high-conversion signals and suppress bids when signals look unfavorable.

Target CPA Worked Example — Web Design Agency

Your product generates €3,000 average contract value. Close rate from lead to client: 20%. Therefore each qualified lead is worth €600 in expected revenue. At a 5:1 ROAS target (spend €1, earn €5), your maximum Target CPA = €600 ÷ 5 = €120/lead.

If your current blended CPA is €85 across all channels, a Target CPA of €95–100 is a reasonable starting point that gives Google room to operate without overpaying.

Budget Constraints and Their Effect on Smart Bidding

Smart Bidding strategies perform best when campaigns are not budget-limited. A "Limited by budget" status means Google is throttling impression delivery to stay within your daily cap — but this also prevents the algorithm from exploring high-conversion opportunities that appear outside its constrained window.

Rule of thumb: your daily budget should be at least 10–20× your Target CPA. If your Target CPA is €80 and your daily budget is €50, the algorithm cannot function — it can only generate 0–1 conversions per day, which isn't enough signal to optimize. Increase budget or raise Target CPA until the "Limited" flag disappears.

Portfolio Bid Strategies

When you run multiple campaigns targeting the same audience or goal, you can group them under a Portfolio Bid Strategy — a shared strategy that optimizes bids across all member campaigns simultaneously. This pools conversion data, helping Smart Bidding learn faster when individual campaigns have low conversion volume.

Portfolio strategies are managed in the Shared Library → Bid Strategies section of Google Ads. They are especially useful when you have several campaigns targeting different countries or niches that all feed into the same lead pipeline.

🚫
Never set Target CPA below your current average CPA on a new campaign

If your average CPA is €120 and you set a Target CPA of €60, Google will severely restrict impressions (it can't find enough conversions at that price) and the campaign will go dark. This is the single most common cause of "my Google Ads stopped spending" complaints. Always set Target CPA at 120–150% of your observed average, then optimize down over weeks.

Section 1.6

Ad Extensions & Assets

Extensions (rebranded as "Assets" by Google in 2023) append extra information to your text ads at no additional cost. They increase the visual footprint of your ad, improve CTR, and contribute to Quality Score through their expected impact component. Google only serves extensions when it predicts they'll help — so adding more options is always beneficial.

Responsive Search Ads (RSAs) — The Foundation

Since 2022, Expanded Text Ads (ETAs) are no longer creatable, and Responsive Search Ads are the only standard Search ad format. An RSA works as follows:

  • You provide up to 15 headlines (30 chars each) and 4 descriptions (90 chars each)
  • Google automatically combines and tests different headline/description combinations
  • Each ad impression shows 2–3 headlines and 2 descriptions
  • Google labels each asset as "Learning," "Low," "Good," or "Best" based on performance
  • You can pin specific headlines to specific positions to ensure critical messages always appear
⚠️
Pinning reduces Google's optimization ability

Pinning a headline to position 1 prevents Google from testing whether other headlines perform better there. Only pin when a headline is legally required (disclaimers), brand-mandatory, or you have strong A/B test evidence it's your best performer. Over-pinning turns an RSA back into an ETA — defeating the purpose.

Writing Effective Callouts

Callouts are 25-character phrases that appear after your ad description. They're the most overlooked extension — most advertisers write generic filler. Callouts should be specific differentiators that address purchase objections.

❌ Weak callouts

  • Professional team
  • High quality work
  • Customer focused
  • Many years experience

✅ Strong callouts

  • Proposal in 24 hours
  • EU GDPR compliant sites
  • No long-term contracts
  • Multilingual support (DE/EN/NL)

Lead Form Extensions for B2B

Lead Form Extensions (also called Lead Form Assets) allow prospects to submit their contact details directly from the ad, without ever visiting your website. Google pre-populates fields from the user's Google account, reducing friction dramatically.

For B2B outreach in the EU/DACH market, Lead Forms work particularly well for:

  • Free website audit offers ("Get a free SEO audit of your site")
  • Consultation booking ("Book a 30-min strategy call")
  • Case study or portfolio download gates

The tradeoff: lead quality from form extensions is typically lower than from website visits, because there's less friction and less qualification. Budget a 20–40% lower close rate versus website-generated leads when calculating your Target CPA for campaigns using Lead Forms.

🔬
A/B testing RSA assets

Once your RSA has accumulated 2,000+ impressions, check the "Asset details" view to see which headlines are rated "Best" by Google. Pause "Low" rated headlines and replace them with new variations. Wait 4 weeks between changes to get statistically meaningful results. Over 6 months, this systematic rotation typically improves CTR by 15–30%.

Negative Keywords — Protecting Your Budget

Negative keywords are instructions telling Google which searches should never trigger your ads. A well-maintained negative keyword list is the single highest-ROI activity in ongoing campaign management — it protects budget, improves CTR (by removing irrelevant impressions), and therefore lifts Quality Score.

Match Types for Negatives

Negative keywords follow the same match type logic as positive keywords, but work in reverse:

Negative typeSyntaxBlocks…When to use
Negative Broad free Any query containing this word or close variants Broad intent block — use sparingly, can block wanted traffic
Negative Phrase -"free website" Queries containing this phrase in order Block a specific intent cluster without collateral damage
Negative Exact -[free website builder] Only that exact query (with close variants) Surgical block of a specific high-spend bad query

Before launching any campaign, create a Shared Negative Keyword List (found in Tools → Shared Library → Negative Keyword Lists) and apply it to every campaign. This master list should cover universally irrelevant traffic that will never convert for any service business:

Campaign-Level Negatives vs. Account-Level Negatives

Beyond the master list, each campaign needs its own negative keyword list tuned to its specific competitive landscape. If you run separate campaigns for "architects" and "law firms," the architects campaign should have legal terms as negatives and vice versa — preventing cross-contamination.

Cross-Campaign Negative Keyword Conflicts

When you run multiple campaigns targeting overlapping keyword themes, Google decides internally which campaign "wins" each auction. If you don't use negatives strategically, your campaigns compete against each other — driving up your own cost.

The fix: use campaign-level negative keywords to carve out clean territories. If Campaign A targets "architects" and Campaign B targets "law firms," add -"law firm" to Campaign A and -"architect" to Campaign B. Now each campaign owns its niche.

The Weekly Mining Workflow

  1. Pull Search Terms report (last 7 days)

    Filter: Campaigns → Keywords → Search Terms. Download to CSV for easier sorting.

  2. Sort by cost descending, identify 0-conversion spend

    Any query spending >1× your Target CPA with 0 conversions is a prime negative candidate.

  3. Check intent manually — don't auto-add

    Some 0-conversion queries are in the normal assist path (early funnel research). Block only clear mismatches.

  4. Add negatives at appropriate level

    Single bad query → Negative Exact at campaign level. Cluster of bad intent → Negative Phrase in shared list.

  5. Track cumulative budget recovered

    Log the spend of each blocked term. After 3 months this figure typically reaches 15–25% of total spend — money redirected to converting queries.

🔬
N-gram analysis for negatives at scale

Download 90 days of Search Terms data. Extract all individual words and bigrams (word pairs) from query strings. Sort by total cost and zero conversions. This surfaces systemic waste patterns (e.g. the word "template" appearing in 40 different queries, all wasting budget) that single-query review misses. A simple Excel pivot table handles this for accounts spending under €10k/month.

Section 1.8

Conversion Tracking Setup — GA4 + Google Ads

Conversion tracking is the nervous system of your Google Ads account. Without it, Smart Bidding is blind, your ROI reporting is guesswork, and you have no way to know which keywords, ads, or audiences are actually generating revenue. Setting it up correctly is non-negotiable.

Two Implementation Paths

Method A — Google Ads Tag (gTag)

  • Install gtag.js snippet on every page
  • Fire conversion event on thank-you page
  • Fast setup — 1–2 hours
  • Single-account scope (only that Ads account)
  • 30-day attribution window by default
  • No cross-device tracking

Method B — GA4 → Import (Recommended)

  • Track conversions in GA4 first
  • Import conversion events into Google Ads
  • Full cross-device, cross-session tracking
  • 540-day look-back vs 90 days with gTag
  • Enables data-driven attribution models
  • One source of truth across all channels

Setting Up GA4 Conversion Events

  1. Install GA4 via Google Tag Manager (GTM)

    Create a GTM container, install the GTM snippet on all pages. Add a GA4 Configuration tag with your Measurement ID (G-XXXXXXXXXX).

  2. Create a conversion trigger

    In GTM: New Tag → GA4 Event → Event name: generate_lead. Trigger: Page View → Page URL contains "/thank-you".

  3. Mark the event as a conversion in GA4

    GA4 Admin → Events → Find generate_lead → toggle "Mark as conversion" to ON.

  4. Link GA4 to Google Ads

    Google Ads → Tools → Linked Accounts → Google Analytics 4 → Link your property.

  5. Import the conversion into Google Ads

    Google Ads → Goals → Conversions → New conversion action → Import → Google Analytics 4 → Select generate_lead → Set as Primary conversion.

  6. Verify with Tag Assistant

    Submit a real test form, check Tag Assistant (Chrome extension), confirm the event fires in GA4 DebugView, and verify it appears in Google Ads Conversion columns within 24 hours.

Enhanced Conversions

Enhanced Conversions is a feature that supplements your existing conversion tags with hashed first-party customer data (email address, phone number). When a user submits your contact form, Google matches the hashed data against its own user graph to attribute conversions that would otherwise be lost due to cookie blocking, ad blockers, or cross-device journeys.

Google estimates Enhanced Conversions can recover 5–15% of previously unmeasured conversions. In markets with high privacy-browser adoption (Germany, Netherlands, Nordic countries), this figure can be significantly higher. Setup takes 30–60 minutes via GTM variables.

Offline Conversion Import — Closing the Loop

Online leads → offline sales is the biggest attribution gap for service businesses. You might generate 50 leads/month from Google Ads, but only 10 become clients — and unless you import the closed deals back into Google Ads, Smart Bidding is optimizing for leads that may never convert.

The technical flow:

  1. Capture the Google Click ID (GCLID) when a lead submits the contact form — store it in your CRM alongside the lead record
  2. When the lead converts to a client, export a CSV with GCLID, conversion time, and deal value
  3. Import the CSV into Google Ads → Goals → Conversions → Upload
  4. Google retroactively attributes the offline conversion to the original click and keyword

With offline import active, switch your Target CPA bidding from "lead" value to "closed deal" value. Smart Bidding will start prioritizing the keywords and audiences that produce clients, not just form fills.

Section 1.9

Remarketing Lists for Search Ads (RLSA)

RLSA lets you modify your search ad behavior based on whether the user has previously visited your website. It's the most cost-effective way to re-engage warm prospects — users who already know you — without switching to expensive Display or YouTube remarketing.

How RLSA Works

Remarketing for Search operates differently from Display remarketing. On Display, you chase users across the web with banner ads. With RLSA, nothing changes visually — you simply adjust your bids upward (or downward, or target exclusively) when a known site visitor triggers a Search auction.

The result: a user who visited your pricing page and is now searching "web design agency DACH region" receives a higher bid from you than a cold visitor, increasing your probability of winning that impression and converting that high-intent return visitor.

The Two RLSA Operating Modes

📊 Observation Mode

  • Your ads show to ALL searchers
  • But you apply a bid adjustment for list members
  • Example: all searchers see your ad, but pricing-page visitors get +40% bid
  • Good for collecting data without limiting reach
  • Set bid adjustments from −90% to +900%

🎯 Target & Bid Mode

  • Your ads ONLY show to users in your lists
  • Dramatically reduces reach — only warm audiences
  • Allows completely different ad copy for returners
  • Best for: form abandoners, high-intent page visitors
  • Requires large enough list size (1,000+ users)

Customer Match — First-Party Data Targeting

Customer Match allows you to upload a list of email addresses (from your CRM, lead database, or email list) directly into Google Ads. Google matches these emails against Google account sign-ins and creates a targetable audience — usable for RLSA, Display, YouTube, and Gmail campaigns.

For the LevelCoding lead pipeline, Customer Match is particularly powerful:

  • Upload all leads from your database as a Customer Match audience
  • Apply this audience to Search campaigns with +50% bid adjustment
  • When a lead you already have in the pipeline searches for your services, you'll win that impression almost every time — reinforcing your outreach with a Google touchpoint
  • Create a "lookalike" (Similar Segments) audience from your converted clients list to find new prospects who resemble your best customers
⚠️
GDPR and Customer Match in the EU

Uploading customer emails to Google for Customer Match requires explicit consent under GDPR. You must have a clear lawful basis (typically consent at time of collection) and a Privacy Policy that discloses data sharing with advertising platforms. In Germany and Austria, enforcement of this requirement is particularly active. Never upload scraped or purchased email lists.

In-Market and Affinity Audiences

Beyond your own website visitors, Google provides pre-built audiences based on observed search and browsing behavior. For B2B service campaigns, the most relevant Google audiences are:

  • In-market: Business Services — users actively searching for business services
  • In-market: Web Design — users researching website design and development
  • In-market: Small Business — users demonstrating small business ownership signals
  • Life Event: Starting a Business — users in the process of forming a business

Apply these as Observation audiences to your Search campaigns. After 30 days, check the Audience segment report to see if any Google-defined audience converts at significantly higher or lower rates than average. Adjust bids accordingly.

Section 1.10

Smart Campaigns vs Expert Mode

Google offers two interfaces for campaign management: Smart Campaigns (simplified, fully automated) and Expert Mode (full professional control). The choice between them determines what data you can access, what optimizations you can make, and ultimately how much control you have over your ad spend.

What Google Doesn't Tell You About Smart Campaigns

When you create a new Google Ads account, Google defaults to Smart Campaigns mode. The interface is clean, setup takes 15 minutes, and the promise is appealing: "Set it and forget it." But Smart Campaigns come with hidden costs that aren't disclosed upfront:

  • No Search Terms Report — you cannot see what actual queries triggered your ads. Ever. Even after switching to Expert Mode, this historical data is gone.
  • No negative keywords — you cannot block irrelevant queries
  • No ad group structure — all your keywords are lumped together
  • No bidding strategy control — Google decides how to bid entirely
  • No audience layering — no RLSA, no Customer Match, no observation segments
  • Very limited ad copy control — you provide assets; Google does what it wants
  • Location targeting is approximate — limited precision compared to Expert Mode
🚫
Never start a client account in Smart Campaigns mode

The moment you create a campaign in Smart mode, you permanently lose access to the Search Terms data from that period. This data is critical for building your negative keyword list and understanding what's driving costs. Switch to Expert Mode before creating any campaign — the "Switch to Expert Mode" link is at the bottom of the campaign creation flow, and Google deliberately makes it small.

How to Switch From Smart to Expert Mode

  1. Go to your Google Ads account overview

    Sign in at ads.google.com. Look at the left navigation — if you see "Overview" and "Campaigns" with a simplified layout, you're in Smart mode.

  2. Click the Tools icon (wrench) or look for "Switch to Expert Mode"

    In some accounts this appears as a link at the bottom of the left navigation. In others it's in Settings → Account Settings → Switch to Expert Mode.

  3. Confirm the switch

    Google will warn that Smart campaign management features will be removed. Confirm. Your existing campaigns are not deleted — they're just now editable in full detail.

  4. Immediately pause Smart Campaigns

    Do not run Smart and Expert campaigns simultaneously — they compete against each other in the same auctions. Rebuild your campaigns from scratch as Expert mode Search campaigns with proper ad group structure.

Google Recommendations — What to Accept and What to Reject

In Expert Mode, Google regularly pushes "Recommendations" with claimed optimization improvements and an "Optimization Score" percentage. This score is gameable and serves Google's revenue interests as much as yours. Treat every recommendation critically:

🔬
The Optimization Score is not neutral

Google's Optimization Score is 0–100% and drops when you dismiss recommendations — even if dismissing them is the right decision. The score is calibrated to push advertisers toward higher spend and more automation. A 72% optimization score with tight negative keywords and correct bidding setup is better than a 95% score achieved by accepting every recommendation. Judge your account by CPA, ROAS, and lead quality — not by Google's internal metric.

Google Ads Scripts — Automation Within Expert Mode

Expert Mode supports Google Ads Scripts — JavaScript code that runs automatically against your account and performs tasks like:

  • Pausing ads when landing pages go offline (URL monitoring)
  • Sending weekly performance summaries via email
  • Logging Quality Score history to Google Sheets
  • Automatically pausing keywords when CPA exceeds a threshold
  • Budget pacing alerts when daily spend is ahead or behind target

Google provides a library of free scripts at developers.google.com/google-ads/scripts. For most accounts, the QS tracker and the broken URL checker are the highest-value scripts to deploy immediately. Both require zero coding knowledge — copy, paste, authorize, schedule.
